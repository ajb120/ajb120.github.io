The challenge

Turn dense weather data into a useful client workflow.

Clients needed a faster way to retrieve past snowstorms without manually working through an undifferentiated archive. At the same time, the main forecasting page carried several time horizons but did not create a clear path across current conditions, 7-day, 14-day, and monthly forecasts.

The design problem was not removing detail. It was deciding what should be visible first, what belonged behind a filter, and how clients could move from discovery to a saved result with confidence.

01

I translated two broad product requests into specific user goals and a sequence the interface could support.

02

Design the information hierarchy

One page, four forecast horizons.

I redesigned the main forecasting page to create a clearer progression from what is happening now to what may happen next. Current conditions establish context; 7-day and 14-day forecasts support near-term planning; the monthly view provides the longer-range outlook.

The key decision was to make the time horizons feel related without flattening their different levels of detail. The structure preserves the information clients rely on while making the page easier to scan.

03

Work directly with leadership

My contribution

I worked directly with the CEO to move from product need to interface structure, balancing client utility with the amount of weather information the product needed to preserve. I contributed to both the design and build of the Historical Event Explorer and redesigned the main forecasting page.

How I made decisions

I used the client's intended tasks as the organizing principle: locate an event, constrain the results, understand what matched, and save it. For the forecast page, I used time horizon and urgency to determine order and emphasis.

Outcome

A clearer route from weather information to a client decision.

The project produced a new way to find, filter, and save historical snowstorms and a more intentional flow across four forecast horizons. It also strengthened my ability to turn an open-ended business request into a structured product experience while collaborating closely with executive leadership.

What I would measure next: time to locate a relevant event, filter usage, saved-event completion, forecast-section engagement, and client confidence after completing each task.
