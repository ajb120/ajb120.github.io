Patagonia Speculative Brand Film

A 32-second AI-assisted concept film developed during my Creative Technology internship at JOAN. The project combined creative direction, scriptwriting, visual-system design, generative production, voice synthesis, and editing.

Creative Technology
2026
Flora, Adobe, Generative AI
Writer, Creator & Editor

Independent speculative concept created for portfolio purposes. Not commissioned by, affiliated with, or endorsed by Patagonia.

01

The creative brief

I set out to create a short brand story that felt emotionally complete, not simply a collection of AI-generated shots. The central challenge was to build a clear narrative while keeping the main character recognizable from scene to scene.

I developed the project at JOAN with feedback from Jesse Stanton. His critiques pushed me to explore more possibilities before committing to an idea—and made iteration a core part of the final result.

02

Writing wide, then narrowing

I began with one script. After Jesse challenged me to write 20 more, I returned with 30 alternatives. We selected five promising directions, revised each through several rounds, chose one, and continued refining it before production.

1 initial script
→
30 new directions
→
5 revised finalists
→
1 final story

What changed: Generating many directions made it easier to compare ideas on their merits. The final script was stronger because selection and revision happened before production—not after the visuals were already built.

03

Designing a repeatable generation workflow

My first attempts revealed that text prompts alone were not enough to maintain continuity. I built a reference-driven workflow that treated each prompt like a production specification.

  1. 01

    Define every scene

    I wrote the setting, composition, subject, lighting, and important visual details for each shot before generating anything.

  2. 02

    Establish the character

    I created a central character image to use as the visual source of truth for the entire film.

  3. 03

    Build consistent scenes

    I generated each scene while referencing the same character image, which helped preserve his appearance across locations and moments.

  4. 04

    Add motion intentionally

    I referenced the approved scene image and paired it with a precise motion description, separating visual design from movement.

04

Voice and final edit

For the narration, I assembled publicly available David Attenborough voice clips as reference material and paired them with the final dialogue in an AI voice workflow. The result was designed to evoke the familiar tone of a nature documentary.

I brought the generated motion clips into Adobe, selected and trimmed the strongest takes, built the sequence, and layered the narration over the edit. This final stage turned individually generated assets into one paced, coherent story.

The narration is synthetic and is not an endorsement or participation by David Attenborough.

05

What I learned

Iteration is creative direction

The 30-script exercise helped me separate attachment to the first idea from the quality of the final idea.

Consistency needs a system

A reusable character reference and approved scene images produced more reliable results than repeatedly rewriting text prompts.

AI still requires authorship

The strongest outcome came from defining the story, setting constraints, evaluating outputs, and editing every component into a deliberate whole.
